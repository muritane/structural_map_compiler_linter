# Reachability Before Foundations

## A Standalone Framework for Support Structures, Reachability Maps, Admissible Transitions, and the Construction of Navigable Reality

---

# Status

This document is standalone.

It does not begin with objects.

It does not begin with matter.

It does not begin with information.

It does not begin with computation.

It does not begin with consciousness.

It does not begin with laws.

It begins with a different observation:

```text
Intelligent systems rarely search directly for things.

They search for ways to reach things.
```

The proposal is:

```text
many foundational projects focus on objects
when they should focus on reachability.
```

Instead of asking:

```text
What is reality made of?
```

ask:

```text
What makes states reachable?
```

---

# Abstract

A common assumption is that reality consists primarily of things.

Examples include:

```text
matter

energy

fields

information

processes

relations
```

The usual strategy is:

```text
identify the correct primitive

derive everything else
```

This framework proposes a different approach.

Many successful systems do not operate by searching directly for objects.

They operate by constructing maps.

Examples include:

```text
navigation systems

robot planners

SLAM systems

scientific models

human cognition
```

The crucial question becomes:

```text
What allows a map to exist?
```

A map requires:

```text
distinctions

persistent landmarks

admissible transitions

recoverable structure

stable correspondences
```

The framework therefore shifts attention away from objects and toward the support conditions required for reachability itself.

---

# 0. The Route Is Not The Map

Consider a navigation system.

When asked:

```text
How do I get there?
```

the system does not store every route.

Instead it maintains:

```text
locations

connectivity

costs

constraints
```

Routes are generated from these structures.

The route is not primary.

The map is primary.

---

# 1. Reachability Before Objects

Suppose a system seeks:

```text
food tomorrow
```

A naive search asks:

```text
Where is food?
```

A deeper search asks:

```text
What allows food to remain available tomorrow?
```

The answer may involve:

```text
refrigeration

storage

preservation

distribution

containment
```

The search shifts from:

```text
objects
```

to:

```text
support structures
```

---

# 2. The Failure of Direct Search

Many searches are performed incorrectly.

People often search for:

```text
labels

categories

objects
```

when they actually seek:

```text
capabilities

outcomes

reachable states
```

For example:

```text
car
```

may actually mean:

```text
reliable transportation
```

Likewise:

```text
information
```

may actually mean:

```text
preserved distinction
```

The search target and the underlying objective are not identical.

---

# 3. Reachability Graphs

A useful abstraction is:

```text
State

Transition

Constraint
```

A state is:

```text
a distinguishable configuration
```

A transition is:

```text
an admissible transformation
```

A constraint is:

```text
something that permits
or forbids transitions
```

Reality may therefore be viewed as a reachability structure.

Not merely a collection of objects.

---

# 4. Why Persistence Matters

Maps require landmarks.

Without landmarks:

```text
navigation fails
```

Likewise:

```text
knowledge

identity

measurement

memory
```

require stable reference points.

If all distinctions disappear immediately:

```text
no map can form
```

Persistence is therefore not merely useful.

Persistence enables navigation through state space.

---

# 5. Correspondence As Map Accuracy

A map succeeds when:

```text
the map corresponds
to the terrain
```

Knowledge functions similarly.

Repeated success implies:

```text
reality permits correspondence
```

Otherwise:

```text
predictions fail

measurements drift

navigation collapses
```

Correspondence is therefore evidence that reality supports map construction.

---

# 6. SLAM As A General Pattern

A SLAM agent does not begin with a map.

It begins with:

```text
observations

motion estimates

partial correspondences
```

From these it gradually constructs:

```text
a navigable representation
```

Human cognition may function similarly.

Science may function similarly.

Knowledge accumulation may function similarly.

The common activity is:

```text
map construction
```

rather than object collection.

---

# 7. Inverse Reachability

Traditional explanation often proceeds forward.

```text
primitive

↓

derive reality
```

This framework proceeds backward.

```text
observed capability

↓

infer support conditions
```

Examples:

```text
knowledge exists

↓

recoverability exists
```

```text
identity exists

↓

persistence exists
```

```text
navigation exists

↓

stable correspondence exists
```

This is a reachability inversion.

Not:

```text
What causes X?
```

but:

```text
What makes X reachable?
```

---

# 8. Admissibility Before Entities

Objects may not be primary.

Instead:

```text
admissible transitions
```

may be primary.

An object can then be viewed as:

```text
a stable region
within a transition landscape
```

What matters first is not:

```text
What exists?
```

but:

```text
What can persist?

What can transform?

What can remain identifiable?
```

---

# 9. Support Structures

Recurring support structures appear across domains.

Examples include:

```text
distinction

persistence

recoverability

correspondence

admissible transformation
```

Remove enough of these and:

```text
maps fail

navigation fails

knowledge fails

identity fails

planning fails
```

The exact list may evolve.

The method remains the same.

---

# 10. Reality As A Navigable Space

The traditional picture is:

```text
objects

↓

relations

↓

knowledge
```

This framework suggests:

```text
support structures

↓

maps

↓

navigation

↓

knowledge
```

Reality becomes understandable as a space of reachable states rather than merely a collection of things.

---

# Final Compression

The traditional question is:

```text
What is reality made of?
```

The proposed question is:

```text
What makes reality navigable?
```

Not:

```text
What is the ultimate object?
```

but:

```text
What support structures allow
stable maps,
reachable states,
persistent identities,
and accumulated knowledge?
```

And therefore:

> Intelligence may depend less on finding paths than on constructing reusable maps.

> Knowledge may be navigation performed on accumulated reachability structures.

> Objects may be less fundamental than the conditions that make them stable landmarks.

> Before asking what reality contains, ask what makes reality navigable.
